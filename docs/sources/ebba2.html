What it is

The second European Breeding Bird Atlas, published in 2020 by the European Bird Census Council (EBCC) with Lynx Edicions, maps the distribution, abundance and change of Europe’s breeding birds from fieldwork carried out mostly between 2013 and 2017 by some 120,000 fieldworkers in 48 countries. It offers two kinds of map: observed abundance and breeding likelihood at 50 × 50 km, and modelled probability of occurrence at 10 × 10 km. An online version followed in 2021.

Kosovo coverage check

The EBBA2 data could not be checked without a request. What can be checked is whether its predecessor is already in this project’s data. The first EBCC atlas (1997, fieldwork 1972–1995) is published on GBIF.

Show the GBIF check 
if (!file.exists(cache)) {
  key <- "c779b049-28f3-4daf-bbf4-0a40830819b6"   # EBCC Atlas of European Breeding Birds
  count <- function(q) tryCatch(
    jsonlite::fromJSON(sprintf("https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?datasetKey=%s&limit=0&%s",
                               key, q))$count, error = function(e) NA)
  reg <- read.csv(file.path(sources_root(), "data", "dataset_registry.csv"))
  write.csv(data.frame(
    check = c("Records coded XK", "Records inside GADM XKO", "In this project's GBIF download"),
    value = c(fmt_int(count("country=XK")), fmt_int(count("gadmGid=XKO")),
              ifelse(key %in% reg$datasetKey, "yes", "no"))),
    cache, row.names = FALSE)
}
knitr::kable(read.csv(cache), col.names = c("Check (EBCC atlas 1997 on GBIF)", "Result"))

What it adds for Natura 2000 in Kosovo

Nine in ten of the 28,785 bird records in the report’s GBIF extract come from eBird checklists, and they cluster where observers go. An atlas is a systematic survey with breeding evidence in every square, which is what an SPA case needs to separate breeding from passage birds (see question 6). The 10 km modelled maps would also give a first, consistent distribution for Annex I species. At 50 km and 10 km the data serve the national screening tier.

Caveats and sensitivity

  • Access terms are unconfirmed; the claim of freely licensed 50 km data from the desk review could not be verified.
  • Modelled 10 km maps are predictions, not observations.
  • Atlas data must be shown at their native grid; finer locations for sensitive species must not be derived from them.

Integration into the pipeline

Low effort once a squares-by-species table arrives: a proposed read_atlas_squares(path, grid_km = 50) would join it to a grid clipped to Kosovo and add an atlas layer to the report’s bird section. pipeline.R is not changed.
