Data sources

Candidate datasets for identifying Natura 2000 sites in Kosovo, each checked against its custodian and, where the data could be reached, against Kosovo itself.

The report is built on one GBIF download, cleaned against an OpenStreetMap outline, and set against the EEA’s register of Kosovo’s protected areas, the EU annex lists, the global Red List and the Union list of invasive species. This section looks beyond them. Each page below takes one candidate dataset from a desk review of what else could inform Natura 2000 site identification in Kosovo*, checks what its custodian actually publishes, and, where the data could be reached, runs a check against Kosovo’s territory. The pages are written so that each can be read, re-run and completed on its own.

The rule throughout is that a value is shown only if it was verified. Every filled field in the registry has a row in sources/registry_evidence.csv naming the page it was checked against and the date; a field without one is blanked automatically and appears as “— to be completed”. A gap is not an error; a guess would be. The precision tiers named below (national screening, site-level delineation, Standard Data Form) are those defined on the Natura 2000 page.

* This designation is without prejudice to positions on status, and is in line with UNSCR 1244/1999 and the ICJ Opinion on the Kosovo declaration of independence.

The 47 datasets stand as follows: Complete: 9; Partial: 19; Documented only: 12; Awaiting request: 4; Lead, not verified: 3; Not applicable to Kosovo: 0. Across all of them, 60 per cent of the verifiable registry fields are filled.

What the checks found

Four findings cut across the pages and are worth knowing before reading any one of them.

  • Kosovo hides under other codes. The same territory appears as XK (GBIF), XKO (GADM and geodata), KS (EFFIS) and Ko (Euro+Med), and inside RS where European data were compiled with Serbia: Serbia’s candidate Emerald sites cover about a fifth of Kosovo, BirdLife holds Kosovo’s IBAs in its Serbia inventory, GISCO has no Kosovo feature at all, and EFFIS files some Kosovo fires under RS. Any query by a single country code undercounts.
  • The EU’s own reporting stops about 50 km away. Kosovo’s four neighbours are outside the EU, so the nearest Article 17 and Article 12 cells are in Bulgaria.
  • European habitat models do cover Kosovo. The EUNIS level-3 maps and the ecosystem-type map have no gaps inside the outline, which makes them the closest thing to the Annex I map Kosovo lacks.
  • Much of what Kosovo knows is in PDFs. The red books, the protected-area table and the park plans are published, but not as data; and the national protected-area table and the EEA register use different code prefixes, so they cannot be joined without translation.

Data gaps

Some of what a Natura 2000 proposal needs does not exist in any of the sources above. Each gap is stated with why it matters for the Standard Data Form (SDF) and who could close it.

1. No Annex I habitat map or inventory

Missing: a map of Annex I habitat types for Kosovo. The EUNIS model and the report’s CORINE screen say where to look, not what is there. For the SDF: section 3.1 needs each habitat’s cover, representativity and conservation, which only mapped polygons provide (question 3). Who: the Kosovo Institute for Nature Protection with University of Prishtina botanists, by field mapping at 1:10,000, starting in the pilot sites.

2. No official or draft Natura 2000 or Emerald site list with boundaries

Missing: any candidate site list for Kosovo. The 2008 identification (Mustafa) is known only from citations, and Kosovo is not a Bern Convention Party, so it has no Emerald list of its own. For the SDF: there is nothing yet to fill one for. Who: the Ministry and KINP, which the NBSAP makes responsible for the ecological network.

3. No confirmed IBA or KBA boundaries

Missing: the digital boundaries of the four IBAs that concern Kosovo; only centroids are public. For the SDF: SPA proposals start from the IBA inventory, and how much of each IBA lies in Kosovo is unknown. Who: a request to the World Database of KBAs, whose terms come with the request form.

4. No published national species monitoring data

Missing: surveillance data with method and effort. The red books of fauna and flora assess species but do not release their localities as data. For the SDF: section 3.2’s population and data-quality fields need surveyed records, not casual ones. Who: KEPA and KINP, with the red-book authors; a data-use agreement could release localities at a generalised resolution.

5. No population-size data for SDF section 3.2

Missing: population estimates with units (pairs, individuals, localities) for Annex II species and Annex I birds. The IBA factsheets give a few, several from 2008–2013 or earlier. For the SDF: section 3.2 requires minimum and maximum population with a unit and a data-quality code. Who: standardised counts in the pilot sites (question 6); the eBird Basic Dataset for effort-based bird data.

6. No wetland, peatland, cave or grassland inventories

Missing: national inventories of the habitat groups most likely to hold priority Annex I types: wetlands and mires, caves, dry and mountain grasslands. For the SDF: these are small or scattered habitats that land-cover data miss at a 25 ha minimum unit. Who: KINP with specialists; the natural monuments in the national table, many of them springs and caves, are a starting list.

7. No Article 17-style range assessments, and no delineation of biogeographical regions within Kosovo

Missing: national range and distribution maps on the 10 km grid, as EU countries report under Article 17, and a Kosovo delineation of the biogeographical regions finer than the European 1:1,000,000 line. For the SDF: a site’s contribution is judged against the national population and range in its region. Who: KINP, as the NBSAP foresees; the European layer is the starting point.

8. Scattered evidence for Annex II invertebrates and freshwater fish

Missing: systematic data for the Annex II groups least covered by casual recording. The data papers add caddisflies and plants but no Annex II list; the taxon atlases are unverified. For the SDF: without them, sites cannot be proposed for these species at all. Who: university specialists, with targeted surveys in the rivers the hydropower inventory shows under pressure.

How this section is maintained

  • sources/registry.csv holds one row per dataset; sources/registry_evidence.csv one row per verified value. Edit both together.
  • R/sources_helpers.R reads them, blanks unverified values, and draws the tables, badges and checklists.
  • Pages cache what they download under data/sources/<slug>/. Small open extracts are committed; PDFs and anything over 50 MB are not.
  • This folder renders with freeze: auto. After editing the registry or the helpers, run quarto render sources/ so every page picks up the change.
