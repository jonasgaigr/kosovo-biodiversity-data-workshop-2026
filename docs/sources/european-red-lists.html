What it is

The European Red Lists assess the extinction risk of more than 10,000 European species, from mammals and birds to bees, grasshoppers and ferns, under the IUCN guidelines for regional assessment, at two levels: the EU and pan-Europe. The EEA publishes the results as tables on its datahub; the current series covers assessments from 2009 to 2022, and the dataset’s owner is the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Environment.

For Kosovo, the pan-European level is the relevant one, and whether its assessments count Kosovo as a country of occurrence is the open question. A related finding bears on it. The 2024 Scientific Data database of European national red lists for vascular plants (Lončarević et al.) treats Kosovo as a country with a red list of its own. Its authors removed the Kosovo taxa from Serbia’s red book of 1999, and did not add them to the Kosovo list.

What it adds for Natura 2000 in Kosovo

The report already carries each species’ global IUCN category. A European category is often more telling for Natura 2000: a species of Least Concern globally can be threatened in Europe, and the Habitats Directive is a European instrument. Where the assessments list countries of occurrence, they also give a European view of which species occur in Kosovo. It serves the national tier.

Caveats and sensitivity

  • Assessment years range over more than a decade; categories may be dated.
  • European and EU27 categories differ; the report should say which it uses.
  • No sensitive content.

Integration into the pipeline

Low effort. A proposed fetch_european_red_list(path) would read the EEA table and join it by GBIF key, adding europeanRedListCategory beside the existing iucnRedListCategory, just as fetch_iucn_categories() does for the global list. pipeline.R is not changed.
